    Neurological indeterminism: On quantum and quantum-like information processing, neurological and human

    This paper suggests a new quantum-like approach to cellular brain dynamics. This approach is related to quantum theory in the way not considered previously, either in strictly quantum or quantum-like approaches to consciousness, two main approaches that relate the workings of brain to quantum theory. The present approach is grounded in the general philosophical position based on the concept of “reality without realism” (RWR), the position advanced by this author previously, along with the corresponding (RWR) interpretation of quantum mechanics (QM). The RWR view assumes that the ultimate reality responsible for the phenomena considered by a given theory, such as quantum phenomena, but possibly also those defined by cellular interactions are beyond representation or even conception. This view also changes the nature of causality and probability in quantum theory and quantum information theory vs. classical physics and classical information theory. Classically, the recourse to probability is merely practical due to our insufficient but in principle possible knowledge concerning the complex systems considered, while the elementary constituents of such systems, say, the molecules of a gas, behave classically causally. Their state at a given point fully determines, ontologically, their state at all other points, and, if considered independently, they could be predicted ideally exactly, deterministically. Determinism is defined here epistemologically by the possibility of such ideally exact predictions. In quantum theory, in RWR interpretations, the recourse to probability arises because no knowledge or even conception of how quantum phenomena come about is in principle possible, and all predictions, even those concerning the simplest possible quantum systems, are probabilistic. This situation defines a radical form of indeterminism, which, as I argue, may be applicable to the neurology of the brain, considered neurologically, vs. quantum-like approaches bracket, “black-box,” the material workings of the brain entirely and deal, by using the mathematics of QM (or rarely, QFT), with the probabilities of observed events of consciousness or thinking. The question may, nevertheless, still be asked: What kind of organization of the brain is responsible for this situation? One answer would be to assume, as Penrose and others did, that consciousness and thinking are effects of certain specific quantum processes (possibly, as in Penrose, even involving gravity, for which there is no quantum theory, thus far) defining cellular processes in the brain. The view I would like to advance is different. In this view, the character of the cellular brain dynamics is neurologically quantum-like, thus, unlike quantum-like theories, in taking the neurological workings of the brain into account, in the character of its effects, without being physically quantum, thus, unlike to physically quantum approaches to the brain. Among its additional advantages is allowing more easily for extending the mathematical formalism necessary for handling of these effects from that of QM to that of QFT, possibly the Yang-Mills theory, a form of QFT that handles the Standard Model of elementary particle physics.

    About the speaker

    Arkady Plotnitsky is Distinguished Professor of English and Philosophy and Literature program at Purdue University. He is the author of ten monographs and several edited volumes, and three hundred articles on foundations of quantum theory, lthe philosophy and history of mathematics and science, continental philosophy, literature, and on the relationships among these fields. His latest books are, Logos and Alogon: Thinkable and unthinkable in mathematics, from the ancients to the moderns (2022), and a co-edited volume (with E. Haven), The quantum-lie revolution (2023). He gave several hundred lectures and conference presentations (including plenaries) and held numerous honors and fellowships at Purdue and elsewhere, as outlines in his vitae: https://purdue.academia.edu/ArkadyPlotnitsky
