    The Mirage of Consciousness: Force, Presence, and the Wave Mechanics of Self-Observation

    The "hard problem" of consciousness persists largely because first-person phenomenology and third person measurement are treated as irreconcilable stances rather than complementary operations within a single investigative structure. Current empirical methodologies predominantly search for consciousness within localized neurological matter; however, since the biological body consists of 99% empty space, what is empirically measured is not consciousness itself, but the physical contrast generated by wave distortion. This paper proposes a structural shift in neurophenomenology: consciousness is not an emergent physical property, but the fundamental pressure (Force) that distorts the basal wave, creating the mirage of the observable universe. This framework hypothesizes that the human nervous system functions as an integrated biological antenna. Specifically, the thalamic circuitry—comprising the pineal gland, thalamus, hypothalamus, and pituitary—operates as a highly specialized four-point geometric receiver. It exists to decode the fundamental pressure of conscious presence into localized, measurable reality. Therefore, what we observe empirically is the consequence of observer-dependent observability, akin to the well documented phenomenon whereby directing clinical observation toward an autonomic rhythm (such as breathing) fundamentally alters that rhythm's natural baseline. To bridge the theoretical physics with lived phenomenology, this paper introduces a deployable first person heuristic: The Navigating Internal Frequency (N.I.F.) Test. The N.I.F. Test is an active protocol designed to induce conditioned autonomic modification and deliberate state re-induction. By subjecting the biological receiver to recursive, cross-sensory synthesis inquiries (e.g., "If a color had a shape, what would it feel like?"), the cognitive operating system is forced to bypass strict categorical measurement (entropy) and identify structural similarities (homeostasis). This recursive folding process covalently bonds disparate sensory inputs, dynamically decreasing the internal perception of "empty space." By actively rewriting the base neurological source code, the N.I.F. Test provides structural, experiential evidence that the observer and the observed are not ontologically separated. The perceived division between 1st-person subjective experience and 3rd-person objective measurement is not Cartesian dualism, but rather a functional bifurcation—a connected polarity stemming from a shared origin. By defining consciousness as the active Force of wave distortion rather than a localized physical byproduct, this framework provides a unified, thermodynamic pathway to resolve the hard problem. Through a series of basic, universally observable case studies and the N.I.F. methodology, this framework demonstrates how reversing internal cognitive entropy effectively aligns subjective presence with objective measurement, proving that consciousness is the unified driver, not the divided result, of the human system. Because each case study relies only on ordinary, reproducible observation rather than specialized apparatus, the findings are independently replicable by any observer.

    About the speaker

    Nicholas "Neutron" Elias Barlas is an independent consciousness researcher specializing in wave mechanics and neurophenomenology. In July 2026, he presented his "Path of Least Resistance" framework at the ASSC Neurophenomenology satellite workshop in Santiago, Chile—a cross-domain model bridging mathematical physics and phenomenological investigation.

    His approach establishes Force/Presence as the fundamental substrate of awareness, with consciousness as the measure of that presence. He distinguishes consciousness (observable even in coma patients) from self-consciousness (meta-awareness and self-observation), arguing that self-consciousness emerges when presence recognizes itself through subjective experience.

    Barlas has authored We Are: The Path of Least Resistance (2026, available online at iamneutron.com), which develops the mathematical foundations across DNA topology, wave mechanics, and consciousness architecture. His work demonstrates how subjective and objective perspectives operate as opposing wave frequencies that, when combined, create an infinity symbol topology—what he terms "subjectively objective reality."

    His methodology centers on universally observable case studies that bridge academic rigor with experiential understanding. These demonstrations include wave propagation, physical separation mechanics, probability observation, and polarity dynamics—each revealing how consciousness functions as presence reacting to itself. The framework offers testable predictions through first-person experimental protocols.

    Barlas's neurodivergent perspective enables him to translate technical concepts across disciplinary boundaries, communicating in what he calls a "bilingual" approach—rigorous enough for academic peer review, accessible enough for direct experiential verification.
