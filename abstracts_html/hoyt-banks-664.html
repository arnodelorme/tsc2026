    Opto-Structural Memory Encoding and Gamma-Band EEG as a Resonance-Envelope Readout

    This theoretical presentation proposes an opto-structural model of memory encoding and recall in which durable biochemical modification tunes the resonant response of cytoskeletal and protein-chromophore networks. The model begins with a standard neurobiological encoding event: voltage-dependent calcium influx activates calcium-sensitive kinase pathways, producing a phosphorylation state P(t). This state shifts a local structural or optical response function according to an effective resonance relation Ωres(P)=Ω0+κpP. Recall is then modeled as selective amplification of the stored resonant state under a structured neural field. The central clarification is a separation of scales. Gamma EEG is not treated as the optical carrier frequency. Instead, gamma-band EEG is modeled as a macroscopic electrophysiological readout of a lower-frequency envelope, beat process, or electrogenic transduction generated by underlying opto-structural resonance. If a(t)=A(t)e−iΩrest denotes a candidate optical or excitonic mode, then the measurable intensity-like envelope is M(t)=∣a(t)∣2. EEG is represented as YEEG(t)=L[Jsyn(t)+λM(t)]+η(t), where L is the volume-conduction/electrode transfer operator, Jsyn is conventional synaptic current, λ is an opto-electric transduction coefficient, and η is noise. The gamma component Yγ=BγYEEG therefore indexes the gamma-band component of M(t), not the optical carrier itself. A damped-oscillator analysis shows that response amplitude is maximized near resonance, with damping setting a finite retrieval bandwidth. The memory trace can be generalized from one scalar phosphorylation variable to a distributed vector of site-specific resonance shifts, yielding a pattern-completion score across coupled structural modes. The framework produces falsifiable predictions: memory-relevant gamma should show coherence with optical-envelope or biophoton-related observables; perturbations of phosphorylation dynamics should alter resonance-dependent recall markers; and candidate cytoskeletal/chromophore modulation should change gamma-envelope coupling without requiring direct Hz-scale optical resonance. This model offers a mathematical bridge among synaptic plasticity, cytoskeletal quantum biology, brain wavefields, and conscious recall. Its purpose is not to assert that EEG directly detects UV or optical carriers, but to formalize the testable hypothesis that EEG gamma can index the macroscopic envelope of opto-structural resonance processes relevant to memory and consciousness.
