    Poster Workshop: Life as Manifestation
    Consciousness, Life, and Evolution According to T-Consciousness Cosmology
    Afshin Lorestani, Mohammad Ali Taheri, Farzad Ahmadkhanlou
    Afshin Lorestani — Cosmointel, Vaughan, Ontario, USA
    Mohammad Ali Taheri — Cosmointel, Vaughan, Ontario, Canada
    Farzad Ahmadkhanlou — University of California at Irvine, Irvine, CA, USA

    Amino acids trace back more than four billion years to the Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA) of all life on Earth. The set of amino acids ultimately adopted by terrestrial life appears to be near-ideal rather than the result of a “frozen accident.” Complementing this perspective, the panspermia hypothesis suggests that simple organic molecules might have arrived on Earth via meteorites or comets; notably, the Murchison meteorite contains amino acids that predate the formation of the solar system. A recent study presented at the University of Arizona further proposes that the conventional sequence in which the 20 essential amino acids emerged from early Earth’s primordial environment may require reevaluation. The authors argue that the stepwise development of the modern genetic code and competition among ancient precursor codes may have occurred simultaneously. T-Consciousness theory introduces a cosmological framework in which the universe consists of both frequency-based and non-frequency-based realms. Within this model, life is believed to have originated in the non-frequency realm through non-organic beings that still exist, before becoming detectable in LUCA’s DNA within the frequency realm. The appearance of life in the frequency realm is attributed to the entanglement of the 20 amino acids, RNA, information, biological dark energy, dark life, consciousness, and other elements spanning both realms. After life became detectable in LUCA’s DNA, it evolved over billions of years—through entangled consciousness, mutation, and natural selection—into plants and brainless animals such as jellyfish, starfish, sea cucumbers, corals, sea anemones, and sponges. Although these organisms lack centralized nervous systems, they can process information, respond to their environment, and even learn and adapt. T-Consciousness theory holds that all living and non-living entities possess entangled components, called minds, in the non-frequency realm that store and process information. Over time, evolutionary processes led to the development of increasingly complex nervous systems in organisms, ultimately culminating in humans. In the frequency realm, biological energy is generated through the conversion of Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) to Adenosine Diphosphate (ADP), a relatively slow process. According to the T-Consciousness theory, when organisms require rapid surges of energy, Biological Dark Energy from the non-frequency realm supplements this need. Cosmointel researchers report that this phenomenon has been tested and verified in standard laboratory environments. The evolutionary trajectory from LUCA to Homo sapiens unfolded both longitudinally and transversely. Longitudinal evolution represents the continuous emergence of species, including humans, and continues to this day. Transverse evolutionary branches arose temporarily to sustain ecological food cycles. These considerations raise fundamental questions: Were the Big Bang, the dominance of matter over antimatter, the formation of fundamental forces, the emergence of the near-optimal amino acid set, the evolution of complex ecosystems, and the appearance of life itself merely a sequence of accidents? The 14-billion-year-old universe operates as a remarkably fine-tuned and coherent system in which all living and non-living components exhibit degrees of consciousness. According to T-Consciousness theory, the probability that this entangled and conscious cosmos—and life within it—arose by accident is zero.
