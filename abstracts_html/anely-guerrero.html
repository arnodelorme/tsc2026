    UNIFIED FIELD RESONANCE AND DIPLOMATIC STABILITY: A Transdisciplinary Research Protocol on Collective Coherence and Peace Agreements

    Contemporary international relations and conflict resolution face a systemic crisis. Despite sophisticated negotiation frameworks and abundant data, peace agreements remain fragile and relapse into violence is recurrent. Mainstream diplomatic models, rooted in materialist and Newtonian ontologies, treat states as isolated actors interacting mechanically, overlooking a fundamental variable: collective consciousness. This research proposes a paradigm shift: collective consciousness is not an epiphenomenon but an active physical field that interacts with social and geopolitical systems. Drawing on Alexander Wendt's Quantum Social Theory, HeartMath Institute's global coherence research, and the Maharishi Effect studies (Global Union of Scientists for Peace), this project hypothesizes that psychophysiological coherence and group meditation can measurably influence the quality and sustainability of peace treaties. The study introduces a three-phase transdisciplinary protocol. Phase 1 conducts a retrospective analysis (1998–2024) of 100 peace agreements (50 successful, 50 failed), correlating diplomatic outcomes with data from the Global Consciousness Project (random number generator deviations) and geomagnetic activity (NOAA/HeartMath GCMS magnetometers), using superposed epoch analysis. Phase 2 applies Natural Language Processing and AI—including topic coherence models (Gensim LDA), semantic similarity (BERT embeddings), and sentiment analysis (VADER)—to quantify "semantic coherence" in treaty texts, testing whether successful agreements exhibit lower linguistic entropy and higher intentional alignment, consistent with quantum collapse of shared wave functions. Phase 3 proposes a prospective intervention study: a controlled field experiment in an active conflict zone, where a global cohort of 5,000–10,000 experienced meditators (aligned with the √1% formula) practices 60-minute daily synchronized Heart Lock-In or TM-Sidhi sessions, monitored via HeartMath Global Coherence App, Inner Balance HRV sensors, and Muse EEG headbands for biometric verification. Outcomes are measured through ACLED conflict data (daily violence events), real-time magnetometer readings, and NLP analysis of diplomatic communiqués, using ARIMA forecasting and Granger causality tests to assess temporal causation. If validated, this research would support that peace is not merely a legal construct but an energetic state that can be cultivated—and that geomagnetic fields mediate collective emotional information relevant to social stability. Ethical safeguards are embedded: intention focuses exclusively on coherence, care, and stability (Heart Lock-In protocol), and coherence is physiologically incompatible with hostile intent. The project adheres to open science principles, with public data, pre-registered protocols, and independent ethics oversight. This protocol offers a rigorous, testable bridge between ancient wisdom and modern technology, exploring whether humanity's most powerful tool for peace resides not in treaties or weapons, but in the resonant coherence of collective consciousness.

    About the speaker

    Anely Guerrero Molina is an international relations researcher and data scientist. She holds a Ph.D. in International Relations and an M.A. in Communication from the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM), alongside advanced training in Data Science and Predictive Analytics. She has a long-standing research collaboration with the University of Tokyo focusing on longitudinal event data capture.

    Currently working at the intersection of international relations, consciousness studies, and social complexity. Proven experience in managing multi-method research projects, tracking civil society dynamics, and analyzing political frameworks (such as the UN Agenda 2030). Specialized in leveraging quantitative tools (Python, SQL, NLP) alongside macro-social theories to analyze collective dynamics and international systems.
