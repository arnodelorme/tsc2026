    Poster
    Somatic Intelligence as a Gateway to Embodied Consciousness: Phenomenological Evidence from LK Intelligent Movement Practice

    The relationship between biological self-regulation and the emergence of conscious awareness remains a central question in consciousness studies. While contemplative traditions have extensively mapped first-person transformations through meditation, and clinical research has documented interoceptive training in therapeutic contexts, there is limited empirical investigation of pedagogical methodologies that cultivate embodied consciousness exclusively through movement and auditory instruction, without meditative stillness or therapist guidance. This paper presents the theoretical framework and preliminary design of a phenomenological investigation into how sustained somatic practice — specifically LK Intelligent Movement (LKMI), a methodology developed since 2009 integrating interoceptive pedagogy, embodied cognition, and perceptual transformation — produces describable shifts in the quality of conscious experience in adult practitioners. The central question guiding this investigation is: what specific changes in the structure of conscious experience do adult practitioners report after sustained LKMI practice, and which pedagogical mechanisms do they identify as responsible for those changes? The proposed design draws on first-person phenomenological accounts (Giorgi, 2009) from adults with at least six months of sustained LKMI practice, complemented by pre-post measurement using the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness (MAIA-2; Mehling et al., 2018). The central hypothesis is that systematic training of interoceptive awareness — the body's capacity to attend to its own internal states — constitutes a direct pathway to expanded and transformed states of conscious experience. LKMI's pedagogical architecture offers a unique research context: instruction delivered exclusively through audio eliminates visual imitation and forces interoceptive inquiry; systematic use of open attention questions sustains non-evaluative present-moment awareness; and a principle of translating horizontal somatic states into vertical lived experience anchors transformation in daily life. Preliminary practitioner accounts suggest transformations across four dimensions: reorganization of bodily self-perception, increased emotional regulatory flexibility, reduction of default-mode mental noise, and sustained attentional presence that transfers beyond the practice context. We propose that somatic education methodologies of this kind offer an underexplored empirical window into the biology-consciousness interface — grounded not in laboratory conditions but in the lived, repeatable, pedagogically structured experience of practitioners. This work contributes to the emerging field of somatic phenomenology and opens dialogue between consciousness research, adult learning science, and the neuroscience of interoception (Craig, 2002; Damasio, 1994; Varela, Thompson & Rosch, 1991).

    About the speaker

    Lea Kaufman is a somatic educator, researcher, author, and founder of LK Intelligent Movement (LKMI), a methodology practiced by professionals in more than 25 countries. Her work integrates embodied cognition, interoceptive pedagogy, and perceptual transformation into a coherent somatic methodology that bridges biological self-regulation and conscious experience.

    Her academic formation is transdisciplinary and extensive. She holds a 5-year degree in Systems Engineering from UDELAR (Universidad de la República, Uruguay), a pre-Bologna program formally equivalent to a Master's degree at Level 7 of the European Qualifications Framework. This rigorous scientific formation grounds her approach to research design, systems thinking, and structural analysis of complex pedagogical methodologies.

    She also holds a 4-year degree from EMAD (Escuela Municipal de Arte Dramático), one of the most prestigious schools of dramatic arts in the Spanish-speaking world, representing approximately 180–240 ECTS in artistic research, embodied practice, and performance pedagogy. This formation shaped her understanding of presence, attention, and the body as a site of knowledge — cornerstones of the LKMI methodology.

    Her somatic training includes an 800-hour International Training in the Feldenkrais Method at the Faculty of Medicine of the University of Colima (UCOL), Mexico, equivalent to 32 ECTS. She has since reached the status of Assistant Trainer in the Feldenkrais Method, a designation that involved additional advanced pedagogical hours. This cumulative academic trajectory significantly exceeds the 300 ECTS threshold required for doctoral admission.

    Kaufman has served as faculty at the University of Colima in Mexico, where she taught somatic education at the Faculty of Dance and the Faculty of Physical Education, and at the Instituto Universitario Autónomo del Sur in Uruguay, where she taught discrete mathematics, logic, and theory of programming languages. This dual academic background — formal sciences and somatic pedagogy — grounds her transdisciplinary approach to consciousness research.

    She is the author of three published books: Apodérate de tu cuerpo (Sirio, 2014), El Puente a la Consciencia (2022), and Ceremonias (Urano, 2024). El Puente a la Consciencia explores the body as a bridge to conscious experience and grounds the theoretical framework of LKMI, while Ceremonias extends that framework into ritual, presence, and embodied transformation. All three works have been widely received within the Spanish-speaking somatic and consciousness communities.

    Her current research investigates how systematic interoceptive training through movement produces measurable and describable transformations in conscious experience — contributing to the emerging field of somatic phenomenology at the intersection of consciousness science, adult education, and embodied cognition.
