    The Brain as a Filter: Introducing a Quantum Ground into Integrated Information Theory

    Integrated information theory (IIT) is an influential framework for understanding the link between the brain’s neural structure and consciousness. However, some criticisms suggest that IIT does not truly address the hard problem of consciousness. To address this, I supplement IIT’s purely structural framework with an intrinsic aspect that, following Russellian monism, grounds IIT’s structural framework and provides the basis for conscious experience. I argue that an appropriate intrinsic element is the ontologically fundamental domain of the quantum wave function. This quantum ground interpretation of quantum mechanics views the foundational level of reality as aware potentiality in a high-dimensional space. This move is motivated in part by noting that both the theoretical quantum ground and IIT’s Q-shape require high-dimensional spaces due to their fundamentally integrated nature. Introducing this fundamental reality, or quantum ground, as an intrinsic element within IIT’s framework leads to interpreting the brain as a kind of filter or tuner that supports a range of experience from a more fundamental source of awareness. This proposed framework, filter integrated information theory (FIIT), does not change IIT’s formalism, yet views its structure as grounded in an ontologically deeper source of consciousness. Despite IIT’s limitations, it arguably directs our attention in a helpful direction with its emphasis on integrated information suggesting something about the property of an appropriate intrinsic element for our world. In addition to addressing the hard problem, this approach brings attention to a possible quantum view of the brain. An intriguing direction is to consider how microtubules in the brain, which have been shown to possess quantum properties, may, (as Hammerof has argued) provide the building blocks for a highly organized network. These microtubules may form the basis of an entangled network of quantum processes embedded deeply within the brain’s architecture, and serve to bridge the various cognitive functions of the brain with a deeper quantum ground, which in turn is not only the fundamental source of consciousness, but has the (theoretical) capacity for coordinating the probabilities that underpin the network of quantum processes. This approach also has additional virtues, such as strengthening the theoretical justification for associating the integrated areas of the brain with consciousness. This brain-as-filter proposal arguably has application in the growing field of psychedelic therapy. In that context, the framework offered here provides theoretical support for interpretations of phenomenological accounts under psychedelic influences of genuinely accessing a deeper field of oneness or connection, beyond boundaries. Such understandings of the deeper nature of reality is likely important, not only in therapeutic contexts, but in addressing important questions on the true nature of life’s meaning. However, much work remains to be done on clarifying the nature of quantum processes within the brain, as well as linking this framework with other current filter notions being developed in conjunction with the brain’s default mode processes.

    About the speaker

    I have a PhD in Economics and spent approximately 30 years at the Federal Communication Commission. However, I've become interested in the philosophy of consciousness and quantum mechanics. I've published several papers that explore the links between philosophy of mind, quantum mechanics, and parapsychology.
