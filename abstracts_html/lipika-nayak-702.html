    Cortical Re-Coupling and Somatic Crystallization: A Neurophysiological Analysis of Spontaneous Out-of-Body Experience and Subsequent Vestibular Pathophysiology

    This paper investigates the rare neuro-anatomical and physical intersections between spontaneous altered states of consciousness and acute peripheral vestibular failure. We analyze a first-person case study of a fellowship-trained clinical neurophysiologist who engaged in deep Kundalini meditation in a supine position at 0300 hours, characterized by systemic sensory decoupling and a subjective out-of-body experience (OBE). Neuroanatomically, this state is modeled as a transient electrical functional dissociation at the temporoparietal junction (TPJ), interrupting the binding of vestibular, proprioceptive, and visual data streams. The subjective transition to an expansive plasma-like consciousness was abruptly terminated by an acute cognitive threat response (maternal survival anxiety regarding a dependent child). This survival network activation initiated an immediate sympathetic surge, sending a flood of norepinephrine through the locus coeruleus to trigger rapid insular and TPJ re-coupling, forcefully re-binding self-location to the physical frame. Crucially, this hyper-rapid neural re-entry occurred while the subject remained supine, accompanied by a localized multi-planar sensation of acceleration at the vertex. Somatic crystallization manifested with a latent mechanical trajectory: the immediate post-meditation period remained asymptomatic, but the morning after the event, transitioning from a recumbent posture to an upright stance during prayer triggered severe rotational vertigo, lateral propulsion to the right, and rotatory nystagmus. Clinical examination confirmed acute right posterior semicircular canal canalithiasis (BPPV), resolving rapidly with canalith repositioning maneuvers. We argue that the physical displacement of calcium carbonate otoconia from the utricle was the direct mechanical consequence of the acute kinetic energy shift during the rapid consciousness re-entry, followed by a latent gravitational migration and accumulation of free-floating debris into the posterior canal over a 24-to-30-hour window. This case challenges reductionist epiphenomenalism, suggesting that transitions in conscious processing can exert direct, causal biomechanical forces on physical hardware.
