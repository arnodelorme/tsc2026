    Curist Mind: a metaphysics of healing connecting our inherent and individual purpose with the dynamic ground of reality through Curist Consciousness
    Isbel Enid Cruz-Chaudhry, Francisco Cruz
    Isbel Enid Cruz-Chaudhry — Wake Forest University, Winston-Salem, NC, USA
    Francisco Cruz — Greenbrook Mental Wellness Centers, Plantation, FL, USA

    Curist Mind provides a philosophical lens to forge a path to sustainable and meaningful living, via a re-vision of nature as emerging from a relational theory of minds and matter. It is an integrative philosophy; a metaphysics of healing. Synthesizing dynamic scientific theories and psychoanalytic (and psycho-dynamic) perspective with ancient wisdom traditions, mindfulness and flow-state practices within the healing arts - to mimic and accentuate the fluid, first principles of relational re-structuring that constitute cosmic creativity. It frames our perspective, our Curist consciousness, in powerful ways to help us see, understand and integrate the reiterative, energetic patterns that inform reality through complexity and the sacred movement of Spirit (eros-imbued energy) in our world, as we co-create myriads of internal and external experiences shaping reality. Curist consciousness grows through Spirit-activism. That recognition of the creative act of living through love by which we establish sacred bonds with people and places. Agape, understood as a deep sense of caring, uniting us at all levels, increases our ability to shift onto a sustainable trajectory that incorporates synergy into our lived experiences. As transformative beings, we establish relationships that help us grow into the best version of ourselves in a world where we lead with love. We know caring relationships help reduce violence and injustice in our communities. Relational revision of dynamic, creative reality also grounds nature as inherently essential to our holistic health and development. Curist consciousness centers purpose for mental and emotional well-being, movement for physical wellness, and belonging (Brené Brown) for social and environmental justice. Synergistic connections highlight our co-creative, sacred stance that collectively has the power to heal at every level, by honoring our relationship with a vibrant world and the belief that all individuals and living beings have the right to a healthy life; including expansive, self-sustaining systems such as our oceans and terrestrial ecosystems. Curist Mind reveals the cosmic dance, the flow of Spirit, highlighting new evolutionary ground - the expansion of our mindscapes. Foundational paradoxes that wisdom traditions such as Taoism teaches through poetic language -often the only form of language that appropriately captures rich nuances (Iain McGilchrist) – are best held in tension through transjective (relational) dynamics (John Verveake) that upend dualism. The Platonic ideals of formal spaces shape our existence and participation with the Real. Art and wisdom traditions throughout history and across cultures instinctively tap into this realm beyond. Science is finally making room for these inherent spaces (see the work of Michael Levin). Gifted with an intuitive connection with Beauty and Goodness that generates sacred energy within and around us, we often intuit these ideals through our intimate connection with nature. We also have the awe-inspiring capacity to persevere with grit and lift each other up with grace in challenging times. To help move us into a productive paradigm for ethical, sustainable and intentional living that will help resolve a multiplicity of crises of existential harm, Curist consciousness proves to be an essential missing link to understanding our role as co-creative, conscientious, and curious Spirit activists.

    About the speaker

    I am a metaphysician. A cross-cultural, multidisciplinary scholar. I am a mother, a cancer survivor, a Spirit activist. Now manager of the Wake Forest University Law Library where I have worked for 20 years. Yet prior to becoming a librarian, I began developing a metaphysics of healing while completing my academic career.

    I began school at the University of Miami in environmental science, then moved into the humanities as a transfer student to the University of Florida. I majored in philosophy (primarily continental and ancient) and the history & philosophy of science (primarily the 17th-19th century). Also learning Italian, German and improving my Spanish grammar. Awards and honors included: Presidential High Honors; Golden Key National Honors Society; German Achievement Award; Ronald E. McNair Program Scholar; Presidential Outstanding Senior Award; Two-Year Scholar Award.

    I taught in the Dominican Republic before accepting a full scholarship for interdisciplinary studies at University of Michigan as a PhD candidate in History. After my first year (and my father's terminal diagnosis), I transferred to the Comparative Literature department and settled for a master's degree completing an interdisciplinary study I created - Theories of the Self. During my second year, I studied the Self through the lens of literature, spirituality, psychodynamic theory and psychology. I was greatly influenced by Slavoj Zizek, Jacques Lacan, other deconstructionist theorists; and dug into quantum field theory through David Bohm and Niels Bohr, continuing my deep dive into the philosophy of science in theoretical physics - bringing fractal, mathematical integration into my field of study - enhancing my metaphysical stance. I learned Arabic. Inducted into the International Honor Society of Phi Kappa Phi and Phi Beta Kappa.

    Ready to return to academia after my father's passing, I chose the Philosophy, Science and Religion program at Boston University for a master’s degree in religious studies for its science literature-based training over CIIS in California. I immersed myself in Islamic history (Golden Age, modern) and philosophy of Islamic science through the work of Seyyed Hossein Nasr, along with a broad religious studies. Awarded a certificate in Religion and Science with a focus in neuroscience through Boston Theological Institute. The contours of my metaphysics began to take shape.

    I moved to North Carolina to marry and later completed my third degree MLS in library information science (cum laude 2010). After raising my children and overcoming many life challenges, I began to heal, live and write again. Curist Mind emerged.
