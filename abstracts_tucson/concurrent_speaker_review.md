# TSC 2026 Concurrent Session Speakers — Review and Recommendations

**Prepared for:** TSC 2026 Program Committee

**Date:** May 2026

**Scope:** 101 speakers listed in the concurrent sessions schedule (C1–C32)

## Executive Summary

This report reviews the academic credentials of the 101 speakers currently scheduled for concurrent sessions at TSC 2026. Each speaker was evaluated based on publicly available information, including institutional affiliation, peer-reviewed publication record, citation impact, and h-index.

The Science of Consciousness conference has a long tradition of welcoming diverse perspectives, including topics that fall outside mainstream academic boundaries. This review respects that tradition. Speakers working on unconventional or frontier topics were evaluated on the same criteria as all others, and those who are well-established within their respective communities are recommended for inclusion regardless of topic area.

The primary concern is limited to speakers for whom we could not verify any academic credentials, institutional affiliation, or publication history whatsoever. Including presenters with no identifiable scholarly record risks undermining the overall quality of the program.

Of the 101 speakers reviewed, **54 are recommended** for inclusion based on verified credentials, and **47 require further review** by the program committee.

## Methodology

Each speaker was assessed using a composite score (0–100) based on four components: institutional affiliation (up to 25 points, distinguishing R1 research universities from smaller institutions and independent researchers), peer-reviewed publication count (up to 25 points), total citation count as a measure of scholarly impact (up to 30 points), and h-index (up to 20 points).

Data was gathered through automated web searches, Google Scholar profiles, Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate, and university faculty pages. Where a speaker works on topics that fall outside mainstream academia — such as parapsychology, Vedic perspectives, mediumship research, or alternative healing — their standing within their own research community was taken into account. A speaker with a strong publication record in a niche or frontier field is valued equally to one in a conventional discipline.

Speakers were grouped into tiers: Tier 1 (80–100, leading academics), Tier 2 (60–79, strong academics), Tier 3 (40–59, established researchers), Tier 4 (20–39, emerging or early-career researchers), and Tier 5 (0–19, speakers for whom limited or no credentials could be verified).
