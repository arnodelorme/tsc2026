Waveguide Quantum Electrodynamics: Tryptophans Entangled with Water as Data Qubits in a Microtubule

Akihiro Nishiyama Ph.D.1, Shigenori Tanaka Ph.D.2, Jack Adam Tuszynski Ph.D.3
1Independent Researcher, Takarazuka, Hyogo, Japan.2Kobe University, Kobe, Hyogo, Japan. 3University of Alberta, Edmonton, Alberta, Canada
Abstract

In this talk we investigate waveguide Quantum Electrodynamics (wQED) for the description of tryptophan residues in microtubules representing data qubits for information storage and possibly information processing. We propose a Hamiltonian in wQED and derive Heisenberg equations for qubits and photons. Using the Heisenberg equations, we derive time-evolution equations for the probability of qubits and distribution of photons both at finite temperature. We then demonstrate the resultant sub-radiance with small decay rates, which is required to achieve robust data qubits for information storage by coupling tryptophan residues as containing data qubits with water molecules as Josephson quantum filters (JQFs). We describe an oscillation processes of qubits in a tubulin dimer through propagation of excitations with changing decay rates of JQFs. Data qubits are found to retain initial values by adopting sub-radiant states involving entanglement with water degrees of freedom. We also describe how information transfer in data qubits occurs via optical signals due to parametric resonance effects.

Nested Fractal Reality Framework: A Step Towards A Unifying Consciousness Theory

Reese Greenwood B.S.1, Anthony J Ryals Ph.D.1, Kara R Stuart M.S.1, Riley Petherbridge B.S.1, Manuela Azevedo B.S.1, Whitney Brimhall B.S.1, Diana Towe B.S.1, Calvin Sims Ph.D.1, Jennifer Wadlington B.S.1, Kurt Geggus B.A.2
1University of North Texas, Denton, TX, USA.2Brookdale Senior Living Solutions, Denton, TX, USA
Abstract

Nested Fractal Reality Framework (NFRF) proposes that reality is composed of hierarchically nested systems whose interactions give rise to qualitatively new domains of existence. As systems increase in complexity and their interactions cross threshold conditions, they generate emergent structures with novel capacities; for example, physics giving rise to chemistry, chemistry to biology, and biology to technology. These emergent realms continue to influence one another across scales; human-generated pollution, for instance, simultaneously affects ecological, organismal, and cellular systems, creating feedback loops between systems. Within NFRF, consciousness is conceived as a system-relative, functional experience arising from efficient interaction between the environment and internal parts of a entity. The quality of a system’s perception is determined by the relationships necessary for persistent self–environment coupling. This definition does not imply uniform phenomenology across levels; instead, it identifies structural preconditions under which forms of awareness may emerge whenever a system achieves sufficient complexity, integration, and unification. NFRF provides a structural vocabulary for identifying parallels in emergence, resonance, and self-organization across disciplines. It additionally emphasizes how human perceptual constraints, methodological limits, and conceptual assumptions shape the scientific study of consciousness. By mapping relationships across physical, chemical, biological, and technological domains, NFRF offers a meta-framework capable of incorporating diverse theories of consciousness, including neurobiological, evolutionary, computational, philosophical, and quantum accounts, within a unified nested architecture of reality. NFRF is highly complementary to emerging theories such as Riddle and Schooler’s Nested Observer Window model (NOW), which characterizes layers of awareness within cognitive systems, from non-conscious processing to conscious experience and meta-awareness. NFRF extends this logic by proposing that observerhood emerges fractally when systems cross the requisite threshold of complexity and self–environment integration. In this view, NOW’s observer layers can be interpreted as specific instances of a broader fractal principle. This synthesis positions meta-awareness not as an exclusively human trait, but as a structural possibility that may arise at multiple levels of organization, including biological collectives, ecological networks, and potentially AI systems. A further strength of the NFRF is its ability to integrate theological and contemplative traditions without sacrificing scientific rigor. Concepts such as Indra’s Net and the Taoist microcosm–macrocosm analogy resonate with NFRF’s fractal, nested organization. Rather than treating these traditions as metaphors, NFRF frames them as convergent intuitions about the recursive structure of complex systems, paralleling contemporary ideas in emergence theory, relational ontology, and self-organizing dynamics. This cross-cultural compatibility invites dialogue between scientific, philosophical, and contemplative worldviews. In summary, NFRF offers a conceptually daring yet structurally grounded ontology by (1) identifying threshold mechanisms through which novel forms of organization and awareness emerge; (2) unifying scientific and philosophical theories with contemplative traditions; (3) extending observerhood capabilities beyond biology; and (4) reframing consciousness as a fundamental relational property of reality. Together with complementary models such as NOW, NFRF provides a multi-level, fractally organized account of consciousness capable of sparking interdisciplinary dialogue about whether awareness is not merely a feature of organisms, but a structural principle woven through a self-organizing universe.

Why consciousness exists: A structural argument

Zoran Vukadinovic MD
Independent Researcher, Centennial, Colorado, USA
Abstract

The starting point of the idea is that the mind is ultimately the means whereby it understands itself. As it does so, it forms a representation of itself. Representations are inherently self insufficient. By definition, they neither are nor do they include that which they depict, and therefore, they necessarily omit complete information about how they relate to it, which, after all, is their defining relation. If representations are self insufficient, then the mind, as it understands itself, cannot possibly do so completely. The mind, therefore, necessarily implies an inherent epistemic gap. This gap is not intermittent. There is no moment when the mind steps outside representation and grasps itself directly — no flash of complete self-transparency in which the gap temporarily closes. It is continually present, whether the mind notices it or not. And it is unique. To distinguish multiple gaps would require representing distinctions within the very domain the gap marks as unrepresentable. Finally, the epistemic gap is free of its own knowable content. As such, it is able to coexist with and interpenetrate any knowable content within the mind while simultaneously remaining distinct from it, as in ineffable. The description of the gap resembles what we call conscious experience, which is likewise continually unique and ineffable in the sense that it imbues mind's knowable content with the what it is like quality. The resemblance raises the possibility that conscious experience does not arise because of anything that the mind or the brain does but because it cannot completely understand itself.

From Cognitive Behavior Therapy to Consciousness Behavior Therapy -- Exploring the Clinical Potentials of Consciousness Studies

Rubin R. Naiman PhD
University of Arizona, Tucson, Arizona, USA
Abstract

Cognitive behavior therapy (CBT) has been extensively investigated and deemed effective for a range of psychiatric disorders. Today, CBT is the most common form of psychotherapy practiced in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and other parts of the world. Despite its popularity, there is growing controversy about its empirical support, evidence of its declining effectiveness, and ongoing concerns about its limited scope and mechanistic style. Specialized forms of CBT such as cognitive behavior therapy for insomnia are facing similar challenges. The term ‘cognitive’ in CBT refers to the mental process of thinking and how it relates to belief, emotion and behavior. Germane as it may be, its scope is much too constricted to accommodate the full breadth of human experience. As an alternative, consciousness behavior therapy would expand CBT’s purview to include experiences such as awareness, metacognition, perception, imagination, fantasy, precognition, intuition, sensation, dreaming, daydreaming, awe, stillness, inner peace, ineffability, grogginess, ecstasy and even sleep. Mental health professional as well as public interest in consciousness has been growing in recent years. Research and practices around mindfulness meditation, psychedelic psychotherapy, social neuroscience and clinical dreamwork, for example, have gradually been redefining the scope of psychotherapy. Consciousness behavior therapy practices would draw from a broad range of perspectives including philosophical counseling, William James’ stream of consciousness, Rumi’s theology, Jungian/archetypal psychology, post-materialist sciences, Hindu and Buddhist teachings, embodied cognition, and anthroposophical medicine. Consistent with Andrew Weil’s observation that the humans have an innate need to expand consciousness, evolving principles and practices of consciousness behavior therapy could significantly enhance the basic framework of all psychotherapy.

Derwin Antione Pritchett Bachelor of Science

Abstract

The nature of consciousness has always been intertwined with our sense of identity, both of which we will explore through the lens of Cartesianism. In this article, I strive to establish an approach for human beings to seize control of their identity and their free will through a true and practical definition of common sense. This is an extension of the framework originally presented in Nous: The Common Sense of Humanity, designed to be prescriptive and to outline a path toward a collective consciousness. The article culminates in a proposed methodology for increasing the consciousness of human beings by objectively measuring and enhancing transient hypofrontality, otherwise known as the “flow state”. In the proposed methodology, I seek to optimize the flow state of (everyday) individuals by using Nousian Ethics to interpret the subjective challenges and skills that determine the quality of human experience. Common sense, by (my) definition, helps individuals navigate the open normative order of our increasingly digital society to better locate their life of meaning (ikigai), thereby precipitating time spent in the flow state. Just like most endeavors in life, living as a human being takes practice; there isn’t a single task we perform that isn’t first learned. Therefore, the aggregate performance of all human tasks can also be classified as a skill: the skill of how to live (well). Qualification and quantification of the skill of ‘living well’— which for our case is inseparable from happiness— is the only thing standing in the way of uniting humanity. In other words, we are attempting to heuristically and empirically define what it means to be a “good person”. There is a spectrum of human experience which describes how well we live— from nihilism to eudaimonia— and which can be directly correlated with how good of a person we are. Creating a system for widespread conscientious self-development requires a clear structural approach and goalposts; if effective, it will enable us to become better people and better (insert functional role/skill category here. E.g., plumbers, therapists, stonemasons, communicators etc.) by extension. All qualities and characteristics we possess flow from our humanity— as nothing that exists by nature is habituated to be other than it is— so improving our humanity will intrinsically improve other facets of our lives. All that’s left is to do the work. It is my thesis that by studying various levels of eminence, an AI agent capable of augmenting our human intelligence(s) can be built which allows each person, and society as a whole, to maximize their true (skill) potentials. My goal is to convince readers to believe, as I do, that it is possible to achieve such internal and societal harmony through a (multimodal) Common Sense AI (CSAI).
