    Concurrent Sessions

    How do concurrent sessions run?

    Concurrent sessions take place Monday to Thursday, 5:00–7:05 pm, several in parallel in different rooms. Each session has five talks, so every speaker has a 25-minute slot, questions included. A session chair introduces each speaker and keeps time. Your day, room and running order are on the concurrent sessions page, and the venue map shows every room.

    The first session of each day (MON-1, TUE-1, WED-1, THU-1) is held in the Paradise Ballroom, is streamed live on Zoom and recorded. Sessions in the other rooms are neither streamed nor recorded, and the way you present differs: see the two questions below.

    My concurrent talk is in the Paradise Ballroom. How do I prepare?

    Talks in the Paradise Ballroom run from the room’s own computer, so please upload your slides in advance using the link Abi sent to all Ballroom presenters by email. If you did not receive it or need it again, contact Abi Behar (abibehar@gmail.com).

    Please arrive in the Ballroom 10 to 15 minutes before 5:00 pm, introduce yourself to your session chair, and check that your slides display correctly. Your talk is streamed live to remote participants and the recording is posted on YouTube, usually 30 to 60 days after the conference.

    My concurrent talk is in another room. How do I prepare?

    Please bring your own laptop with your presentation on it, and a copy on a USB flash drive as a backup. The room projectors use HDMI, so bring an adapter if your laptop has no HDMI port.

    Arrive in the room by 4:50 pm, ten minutes before the session, to check the AV. The schedule is tight, so any time needed to fix AV issues with your computer comes out of your own slot. These rooms are not streamed or recorded.

    I am chairing a concurrent session. What does that involve?

    • Arrive 10 to 15 minutes early, check that your speakers are present and that the projector works.
    • Open the session and introduce each speaker briefly, using the information on their abstract page (linked from the concurrent sessions page).
    • Keep time. Each speaker has 25 minutes including questions, roughly 20 minutes of talk and 5 of discussion. Please hold the schedule even when a talk runs short or long: participants move between parallel sessions to hear specific talks.
    • Moderate the questions after each talk and close the session at 7:05 pm.
    • If you also present in the session, ask a colleague to keep time during your own talk.
    In the Paradise Ballroom (MON-1 to THU-1), the session is streamed live and recorded, and talks run from the room’s computer with slides uploaded in advance, so changeovers are quick. Starting and ending on time matters for the stream. In the other rooms, each speaker plugs in their own laptop; if a laptop will not connect, move on to the next speaker and come back, since setup time comes out of the speaker’s own slot.

    Poster Guidelines

    How should I design my poster?

    Keep it visual and concentrate on a few main points — bullets and clear figures work better than dense paragraphs, and your message should be clear even when you are away from the poster. Use large sans-serif lettering (e.g. Arial or Lucida Sans) readable from 1 meter: title about 2″–3″ high, subheadings ½″–1″. Put the title, author names, and affiliations at the top, and let the content flow from the upper left corner, left to right and top to bottom (introduction, methods, results, discussion, summary, references). Do not simply paste up typed pages from a paper.

    How and when do I set up my poster?

    Mount your poster on the day of your session — at 1:30 pm on Tuesday or Wednesday, during the lunch break, so it is up by 2 pm. Place it under the sign for your discipline (Philosophy, Neuroscience, Cognitive Science/Psychology, Physics/Biology, Experiential, or Humanities). Special mounting tape from the poster-board vendor is provided on site: no glue, Velcro, nails, or foam boards, and please do not write or mark on the boards. Remove your poster promptly after your session.

    How should I present my poster?

    A poster session is an informal discussion, not a podium presentation. Be with your poster during your scheduled session, and prepare a quick 1–2 minute summary of your research for visitors. A good way to start a conversation is to point out the figure that shows your core conclusion and let questions flow from there.

    What happens if my poster wins?

    Poster winners are announced during the conference and get the chance to present their work in front of the conference in a dedicated concurrent session: winners of Poster Session 1 (Tuesday) present on Wednesday in WED-6 (Bay View Room), and winners of Poster Session 2 (Wednesday) present on Thursday in THU-7 (Mission Bay Room), both 5:00–7:05 pm. Each winner has 20 minutes to present and take questions.

    Poster presenters are encouraged to bring their poster on a USB thumb drive (PDF or slides) so that it can be projected to the audience. If you are notified that you have won in your discipline, please let us know whether you can attend the Wednesday or Thursday session — email info@tsc2026.org.

    Conference Organization

    How are conference topics organized?

    The program is organized around the conference’s taxonomy of consciousness studies: six main disciplines — Philosophy, Neuroscience, Cognitive Science/Psychology, Physics/Biology, Experiential, and Humanities — each divided into numbered subcategories. Abstracts are submitted under these categories, and talks, posters, and poster-board signs follow them. The full list is available as a PDF: TSC 2026 Taxonomy of Categories.

    What is the relationship between TSC 2026 and the Tucson conferences?

    The Science of Consciousness 2026 (TSC2026) is the 32nd edition of the international interdisciplinary conference on consciousness that began in Tucson, Arizona in 1994 under the name The Science of Consciousness (TSC). Following the cancellation of TSC 2026, the conference has been reorganized under new leadership and governance at UC San Diego. TSC2026 continues the scientific tradition of the series while operating under an independent organizing committee and a new donor and speaker vetting protocol.

    How is gender balance addressed in the program?

    The TSC 2026 Scientific Committee has balanced gender representation, and we are actively working to achieve balanced representation among plenary speakers as well. Ensuring diverse perspectives is a priority for the organizing committee across all aspects of the program.

    How is the conference funded? Who are the sponsors?

    TSC 2026 is funded through registration fees, institutional support, and vetted sponsorships. All potential sponsors undergo our vetting protocol before any funds are accepted. We will publish a list of sponsors on the website as they are confirmed. If you or your organization are interested in sponsoring the conference, please contact us at info@tsc2026.org.

    Why is registration expensive?

    TSC 2026 is a non-profit conference, and registration fees are set to cover costs, not generate profit. The fee covers five full days of programming, including plenary talks, concurrent sessions, workshops, two receptions, food trucks on site every day (attendees pay for their own meals, but the conference pays the resort a fee to allow the trucks on site), and professional filming of talks with videos released freely after the conference. Unlike many conferences of similar scope, TSC 2026 does not charge separately for workshops or social events. For comparison, the 2026 OHBM annual meeting (Organization for Human Brain Mapping) charges over $1,260 for non-member early registration alone, before educational courses or membership fees.

    The negotiated hotel rates at Paradise Point are also well below the resort’s standard pricing — student rooms start at $199/night with the resort fee waived. Students pay a reduced registration fee ($495 early / $595 regular), and a streaming-only option is available at $130 for those who cannot attend in person. See the registration page for full details.

    San Diego

    What is the weather like in October? What should I pack?

    October is one of the best months in San Diego. Expect daytime highs around 24 °C (75 °F) and overnight lows near 16 °C (61 °F), with abundant sunshine and very little rain — October averages well under an inch for the whole month. Mornings can start with coastal cloud that burns off by late morning.

    Daytime dress is light: the walk between session rooms is outdoors, across the resort’s gardens. The conference rooms themselves are air-conditioned, so a light layer is useful indoors if you feel the cold.

    The one thing people underestimate is the evening by the water. Paradise Point sits on Mission Bay, and after sunset the breeze off the water makes it noticeably cooler than the daytime forecast suggests. For the outdoor evening events — the beach bonfire and hangout, the Fire Show dinner on the terrace, and anything else on the sand — bring a sweater or a light jacket. Ocean water temperature is around 20 °C (68 °F), comfortable for a swim for most people.
