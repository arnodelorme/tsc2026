## Step 1: Identity Resolution

Deepak Chopra (born October 22, 1946, New Delhi, India) is an Indian-American author, physician, and public speaker. He graduated from the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (1969), moved to the United States in 1970, and became board certified in internal medicine with a specialization in endocrinology. He taught at Tufts University, Boston University, and Harvard Medical School, and served as chief of staff at New England Memorial Hospital.

In 1985, Chopra shifted from conventional medicine to alternative and integrative medicine after meeting Maharishi Mahesh Yogi. He became founding president of the American Association of Ayurvedic Medicine. His 1993 book *Ageless Body, Timeless Mind* became a bestseller after an appearance on The Oprah Winfrey Show, establishing him as a leading figure in the wellness industry.

Chopra has authored more than 80 books (22 New York Times bestsellers), translated into 43+ languages. He is a Fellow of the American College of Physicians. In 1996, he co-founded the Chopra Center for Wellbeing in Carlsbad, California. He subsequently founded The Chopra Foundation (a nonprofit) and Chopra Global (a wellness company).

At UCSD, Chopra held a Voluntary Clinical Professor (without salary) appointment in the School of Medicine's Department of Family Medicine and Public Health, beginning in 2016. Following the Epstein file revelations, UCSD announced his appointment would end June 30, 2026, and that he has no active responsibilities.

He is married to Rita Chopra (since 1970). They have two children: Gotham Chopra (filmmaker) and Mallika Chopra (author). Based in California.

Public profiles: Wikipedia, personal website (deepakchopra.com), X (@DeepakChopra), LinkedIn

## Step 2: Criminal and Legal History

No criminal charges have been filed against Deepak Chopra. However, there is a notable history of civil litigation:

### As Defendant

- **Sexual harassment lawsuit (1996, San Diego Superior Court):** A former employee alleged Chopra made "numerous unwelcome sexual overtures" and that she had to answer calls from women with whom Chopra was having affairs. Chopra denied the allegations and counter-sued for blackmail. A jury unanimously found against Chopra's blackmail claim. In a subsequent trial on the employee's claims of retaliation and wrongful termination, a jury unanimously rejected the lawsuit in Chopra's favor. The sexual harassment claim itself was dismissed.

- **Wrongful death lawsuit:** The wife of a man with leukemia who had adopted Chopra's practices and later died sued Chopra. The suit was dismissed.

- **Michael Riley v. Deepak Chopra (9th Circuit, 2022):** Case decided by the U.S. Court of Appeals. Details limited in available sources.

### As Plaintiff

- **$35 million defamation suit against the Weekly Standard:** Over a story claiming Chopra had hired a prostitute. The prostitute later recanted; the magazine printed an apology and paid Chopra's legal fees.

- **$30 million defamation suit against JAMA (1991):** Over an article Chopra said mocked his beliefs. Settled for an undisclosed amount.

- **Defamation suit against the Daily Mail:** Over questions about his medical license.

- **Fraud suit against James Patrick Walsh (2013):** Chopra alleged a business partner defrauded him of $155,000 in a "consciousness project."

**Result: FLAG — Extensive civil litigation history. Sexual harassment lawsuit dismissed/defeated, but pattern of litigation is notable.**
