## Step 2: Criminal and Legal History

No criminal charges found. However, there is one significant civil matter:

### Cred Inc. Liquidation Trust v. Winslow Carter Strong

**Case:** 1:22-ap-50134, Delaware Bankruptcy Court, Filed February 17, 2022

**Background:** Cred Inc. was a crypto lending platform that filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2020 with up to $500 million in liabilities. The Cred Liquidation Trust alleged that a transaction with Winslow Strong constituted a fraudulent transfer.

**Allegation:** In early 2020, Strong agreed to loan 500 bitcoin to Cred at 9% interest via CredEarn. The day before that agreement was executed, Cred management offered Strong a different arrangement — purchasing a note from Income Opportunities, a Luxembourg entity described as a "bankruptcy remote entity." Strong purchased the note using the 500 bitcoin. In July 2020, as Cred was heading toward bankruptcy, Cred repurchased the note from Strong for approximately 516 bitcoin (~$4.8 million at the time; ~$21 million at time of lawsuit). The Trust alleged this transfer was fraudulent — that Cred paid 516 BTC in exchange for an essentially worthless bond while insolvent.

**Strong's defense:** Strong characterized the transaction as a legitimate repayment of funds due with interest. He stated that Cred had marketed Income Opportunities as "better-insulated from bankruptcy" and that the purchase was intended to consolidate his exposure to MoKredit (Cred's Chinese lending partner).

**Court rulings:**
- May 2022: The court dismissed two of five counts, including the actual fraud claim.
- February 2023: The remaining claims were settled. The case was dismissed with prejudice (cannot be reopened).

**Strong's statement:** Strong told CoinDesk that he lost more on the settlement and legal fees defending the claim than he earned on the original loan.

**Assessment:** This was a civil adversary proceeding within a bankruptcy case, not a criminal prosecution. The actual fraud count was dismissed by the court. The remaining counts were settled and dismissed with prejudice. The transaction occurred in the context of a chaotic bankruptcy involving multiple parties and a larger alleged fraud by Cred's investment manager. Strong was not the primary wrongdoer in the Cred collapse; however, his receipt of 516 BTC from an insolvent entity raised legitimate legal questions that required judicial resolution.

**Result: FLAG — Civil litigation involving allegations of fraudulent transfer in a bankruptcy proceeding. Actual fraud count dismissed; remaining counts settled.**
